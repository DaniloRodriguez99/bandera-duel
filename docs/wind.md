# Wind animation

`WindManager` adds original procedural pixel foliage animation through one Phaser Graphics object at depth 0.4. Construct it with the scene, call `setEnvironment({ width, height, theme, arena, walls, bushes?, richerArt? })` on map transitions, and call `update(time, delta)` from the scene update. Destroy it when its scene owner is disposed; an idempotent scene shutdown hook is also registered. Environment changes immediately clear prior foliage and actor samples.

Arena bushes get small crowns inside their existing rectangles. Sparse grass tufts frame the outskirts, excluding walls, home columns and the central combat lane. World integration supplies `grassPatches` from actual grass/moss tile metadata, excluding walls, so animation never invents plants on water or roads. Forest wall/canopy edges also receive crowns; settlement/building rectangles are excluded from those walls. Nothing changes collisions, visibility rules, bush hiding, gameplay entities or network state.

Roots stay fixed. A shared slowly changing sinusoidal gust bends upper leaves with spatial phase offsets, producing waves rather than independent jitter. Integer crown displacement is limited to a few pixels, with muted green/gold colors and crisp rectangular leaves. No texture warping or shaders are required. Existing tree and bush artwork remains visible below the overlay.

For the authored forest redesign, `richerArt: true` selects four related green/olive foliage palettes. All maps receive four asymmetric crown/blade silhouettes, varied heights, and deterministic offsets within bush beds instead of an identical sprig grid. These are tiny moving accents over the authored static forest foliage; large trees, roots and entire bush textures never jitter. The overlay needs no separate atlas frames or assets. Crown displacement is capped at three pixels to respect its existing bush inset; open grass can bend up to five.

Call `setActors(readonly {x,y,visible?}[])` with the scene's rendered visible actor positions before `update`. The module filters explicit `visible: false` samples and retains at most 64 actors. The caller must omit concealed opponents: this visual module does not inspect gameplay state or decide visibility. A supplied actor within 28 world pixels bends nearby upper leaves away, easing toward the strongest nearby influence and smoothly returning with a 140ms response constant. No physics bodies or gameplay impulses are created. Camera-cell reselection preserves bend for surviving anchors. Reduced motion freezes both wind and actor response; intensity scales displacement.

The manager uses the existing `visualSettings` controls. High/medium/low quality caps the visible selection at 100/60/28 sprigs; intensity scales both density and displacement. Intensity zero clears it immediately. Reduced motion freezes crowns while preserving their static silhouette. Updates are throttled to roughly 30 Hz, or 15 Hz on low quality, and reduced motion only redraws when settings or the camera window changes. One sprig uses at most seven tiny filled rectangles. Actor influence has a cheap axis-distance rejection before calculating distance. No tweens, particles, physics bodies, textures or per-node game objects are created.

A bounded deterministic selection retains at most 100 anchors near the camera, rebuilding only across 64-pixel camera cells or viewport-size changes. Nodes outside the camera view are culled before drawing. Coordinate hashing never consumes gameplay randomness. There are no drifting-leaf emitters; the budget is spent on anchored foliage so motion remains easy to read.

Validation should inspect forest and crossroads arena bushes, camera scrolling in forest world zones, all quality settings, zero intensity, reduced-motion toggling and repeated zone changes. Confirm roots stay still, moving crowns stay inside bush footprints, combat lanes remain clear and shutdown removes the single Graphics object. Runtime visual review and device performance measurements are integration responsibilities; a compile check alone does not establish visual quality or frame rate.
